# Canonical primary texts — D. W. Winnicott

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Prefer collected papers and authoritative editions.

## Core sequence

1. **“Transitional Objects and Transitional Phenomena”** (1951) — the intermediate area between inner and outer reality, play, attachment, and symbolization.
2. **_Playing and Reality_** (1971) — play, creativity, cultural experience, transitional space, and the conditions under which a self can feel real.
3. **_The Maturational Processes and the Facilitating Environment_** (1965) — dependence, holding, integration, environmental provision, and development.
4. **“Ego Distortion in Terms of True and False Self”** (1960) — compliance, spontaneity, adaptation, and the defensive organization of selfhood.
5. **“The Capacity to Be Alone”** (1958) — solitude as an achievement grounded in reliable presence rather than simple independence.
6. **“Fear of Breakdown”** (written 1963, published posthumously) — catastrophe that has in some sense already happened but has not yet been experienced as such.

## Secondary core

- **“Hate in the Counter-Transference”** (1947).
- **“The Use of an Object and Relating through Identifications”** (1969).
- **_Through Paediatrics to Psycho-Analysis_** (1958).
- **_The Child, the Family, and the Outside World_** (1964).

## Branch question

Use Winnicott here to test whether autonomy rests on prior dependence, whether adaptation has become compliance, whether play is possible, and whether the problem is not hidden desire but the absence of an environment in which experience can become real.
