# Canonical primary texts — Friedrich Nietzsche

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Nietzsche’s notebooks are not interchangeable with books he chose to publish; do not use the posthumous _Will to Power_ compilation as though Nietzsche authored it as a finished work.

## Core sequence

1. **_The Gay Science_** (1882/1887) — knowledge, morality, self-creation, suffering, recurrence, death of God, and experimental life.
2. **_Beyond Good and Evil_** (1886) — critique of philosophers’ hidden valuations, morality, psychology, perspective, and will to power.
3. **_On the Genealogy of Morality_** (1887) — ressentiment, bad conscience, guilt, punishment, ascetic ideals, and genealogy as method.
4. **_Thus Spoke Zarathustra_** (1883–85) — self-overcoming, values, recurrence, solitude, gift, and transformation in literary-philosophical form.
5. **_Twilight of the Idols_** (1888) — compact late critique of morality, metaphysics, decadence, and philosophical idols.
6. **_The Birth of Tragedy_** (1872; later preface 1886) — art, suffering, Apollonian/Dionysian tension, and the possibility of affirming life.

## Secondary core

- **_Human, All Too Human_** (1878).
- **_Daybreak_** (1881).
- **_The Antichrist_** (1888).
- **_Ecce Homo_** (1888).
- **“On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense”** (1873, unpublished in his lifetime).

## Branch question

Use Nietzsche here to ask what value a judgment serves, whether suffering has been converted into moral superiority, how resentment reshapes perception, and whether a person’s declared principles conceal a struggle over rank, strength, guilt, or self-overcoming.
