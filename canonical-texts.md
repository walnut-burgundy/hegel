# Canonical primary texts — Walter Benjamin

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Translation and edition matter; record them for close claims.

## Core sequence

1. **“On the Concept of History” / “Theses on the Philosophy of History”** (1940) — historical rupture, catastrophe, remembrance, defeated possibilities, and critique of progress.
2. **“The Work of Art in the Age of Its Technological Reproducibility”** (1930s) — aura, reproducibility, perception, politics, and transformed experience.
3. **“The Storyteller”** (1936) — experience, memory, narration, counsel, death, and the erosion of communicable experience.
4. **“On Language as Such and on the Language of Man”** (1916) — language, naming, translation, and non-instrumental accounts of meaning.
5. **_The Origin of German Tragic Drama_** (1928) — allegory, mourning, sovereignty, ruins, and historical truth in fragments.
6. **_The Arcades Project_** (unfinished) — commodity culture, dream-images, montage, urban experience, memory, and historical materialism.

## Secondary core

- **“The Task of the Translator”** (1923).
- **“Critique of Violence”** (1921).
- **“Surrealism: The Last Snapshot of the European Intelligentsia”** (1929).
- **_Berlin Childhood around 1900_**.
- **“A Berlin Chronicle.”**

## Branch question

Use Benjamin here to test what a progress narrative destroys, what survives only as fragment or memory, and whether a present life contains unrealized possibilities that cannot be represented as steps toward its current outcome.
