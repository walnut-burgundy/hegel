# Canonical primary texts — Søren Kierkegaard

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Pseudonymous works speak from deliberately different positions; do not flatten all pseudonyms into Kierkegaard’s direct authorial voice.

## Core sequence

1. **_Either/Or_** (1843) — aesthetic and ethical life, choice, boredom, seduction, commitment, and forms of despair before despair is named systematically.
2. **_Fear and Trembling_** (1843, Johannes de Silentio) — faith, anxiety, sacrifice, the ethical, and the singular individual.
3. **_Philosophical Fragments_** (1844, Johannes Climacus) — truth, learner, paradox, moment, and the limits of recollection.
4. **_The Concept of Anxiety_** (1844, Vigilius Haufniensis) — freedom, possibility, anxiety, innocence, and sin.
5. **_Concluding Unscientific Postscript_** (1846, Johannes Climacus) — subjectivity, existence, inwardness, uncertainty, and critique of system.
6. **_The Sickness Unto Death_** (1849, Anti-Climacus) — despair as a disorder in the self’s relation to itself, including not wanting and desperately wanting to be oneself.

## Secondary core

- **_Works of Love_** (1847).
- **_Repetition_** (1843, Constantin Constantius).
- **_Stages on Life’s Way_** (1845).
- **_The Present Age_** (1846).
- **_Practice in Christianity_** (1850, Anti-Climacus).

## Branch question

Use Kierkegaard here to test whether a person is caught not simply between choices but in a relation to possibility, commitment, anxiety, and the impossible demand to become a self without ever possessing a detached view of what that self is.
