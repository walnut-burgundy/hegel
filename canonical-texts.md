# Canonical primary texts — Karl Marx

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Distinguish Marx’s manuscripts, collaborative works, mature economic critique, and later editorial constructions rather than treating them as one undifferentiated doctrine.

## Core sequence

1. **_Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844_** — alienated labor, species-being, private property, need, and estrangement.
2. **_The German Ideology_** (with Friedrich Engels, 1845–46) — material production, social relations, ideology, division of labor, and critique of autonomous consciousness.
3. **_The Communist Manifesto_** (with Engels, 1848) — class struggle, bourgeois transformation, world market, and revolutionary politics.
4. **_The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte_** (1852) — class, political representation, historical repetition, contingency, and inherited forms.
5. **_Grundrisse_** (1857–58) — notebooks on production, circulation, machinery, social forms, and method.
6. **_Capital, Volume I_** (1867) — commodity, value-form, fetishism, exploitation, machinery, accumulation, and the social relations concealed by market appearance.

## Secondary core

- **“Theses on Feuerbach”** (1845).
- **_A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy_** (1859), especially the commodity analysis and Preface.
- **“Value, Price and Profit”** (1865).
- **_Critique of the Gotha Programme_** (1875).
- **_Capital_, Volumes II and III** — indispensable when claims extend beyond Volume I’s level of analysis.

## Branch question

Use Marx here to test whether an apparently personal problem is organized by labor, property, class, markets, institutions, or commodity forms, and whether relations among people are being misrecognized as properties of things or individual character.
