# Canonical primary texts — Frantz Fanon

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Keep Fanon’s clinical, phenomenological, political, and anticolonial registers together rather than reducing him to slogans about violence.

## Core sequence

1. **_Black Skin, White Masks_** (1952) — racialized embodiment, language, recognition, desire, inferiority, projection, and the psychic effects of colonial social relations.
2. **_A Dying Colonialism_** (1959) — social transformation during anticolonial struggle, family, medicine, radio, gender, and everyday institutions.
3. **_The Wretched of the Earth_** (1961) — colonial violence, national consciousness, class formation, decolonization, culture, and the psychological injuries of domination.
4. **“The Fact of Blackness” / “The Lived Experience of the Black Man”** — the phenomenology of being fixed by the racializing gaze; title varies by translation.
5. **“Colonial War and Mental Disorders”** in _The Wretched of the Earth_ — clinical cases showing political violence entering psychic life.
6. **_Toward the African Revolution_** (posthumous, 1964) — journalism, political analysis, colonial medicine, Algeria, Africa, and revolutionary strategy.

## Secondary core

- **“Medicine and Colonialism.”**
- **“Racism and Culture.”**
- Fanon’s psychiatric writings from Blida-Joinville and Saint-Alban, where available in scholarly editions.

## Branch question

Use Fanon here to test whether a psychological description is silently treating domination as an individual pathology, whether recognition is poisoned by a racial or colonial structure, and how material violence becomes lived bodily and psychic experience.
