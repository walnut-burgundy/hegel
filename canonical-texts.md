# Canonical primary texts — Simone de Beauvoir

Use these as the primary textual shelf for this branch. Prefer authoritative French editions or reputable translations; note translator and edition when wording matters.

## Core sequence

1. **_The Ethics of Ambiguity_** (1947) — freedom as situated, reciprocal, risky, and inseparable from the freedom of others.
2. **_The Second Sex_** (1949) — becoming, embodiment, immanence/transcendence, alterity, social construction, dependency, and the material organization of gender.
3. **_She Came to Stay_** (1943) — freedom, jealousy, possession, recognition, and the difficulty of accepting another consciousness as genuinely free.
4. **_Memoirs of a Dutiful Daughter_** (1958) — formation of a self through family, class, education, rebellion, and retrospective narration.
5. **_The Prime of Life_** (1960) — intellectual vocation, relationships, history, and the construction of a life through choices made under conditions not fully chosen.
6. **_The Coming of Age_ / _Old Age_** (1970) — aging as lived experience and as a social condition structured by institutions, labor, poverty, and exclusion.

## Secondary core

- **_Pyrrhus and Cineas_** (1944).
- **_All Men Are Mortal_** (1946).
- **_A Very Easy Death_** (1964).
- **_Adieux: A Farewell to Sartre_** (1981).

## Branch question

Use Beauvoir here to test whether an abstract appeal to freedom ignores situation, dependence, embodiment, age, social role, or another person’s equally real freedom—and whether a person is being confined to an identity imposed from outside.
